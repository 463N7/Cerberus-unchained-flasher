03 / GPS ADD-ON

Add GPS to your Cerberus.

REV144 · UART NMEA · RECEIVE ONLY

Give your wardriving logs a location. Connect a serial GNSS receiver to the ESP32-S3 using the pinout below, then choose its profile on Cerberus.

GPS pinout at a glance.

Default GPS connections. Match the supply voltage and UART/PPS signal levels to your exact GPS module or breakout.
GNSS breakoutCerberus ESP32-S3
VCC / VINPower3V3 or 5VUse 3.3 V or 5 V according to your GPS board's power requirements. Use 5 V only if its power input is rated for 5 V.
GNDGroundGNDUse a common ground between both boards.
TX / TXDReceiver data outGPIO1 · S3 RXGPS TX → Cerberus RX. Level-shift if needed.
RX / RXDReceiver data inGPIO2 · S3 TXCerberus TX → GPS RX. Optional; rev144 does not send receiver commands.

These are GPIO labels, not connector position numbers. Find the matching pads using your Cerberus PCB labels or schematic; header layouts can differ.

Wire it. Select it. Find a fix.

  1. Power off before wiring.Disconnect USB and battery power. Use 3V3 or 5V for power according to your GPS board's requirements, and match its UART/PPS signal levels separately. Never feed 5 V or RS-232 signals into an S3 GPIO.
  2. Open Settings → GPS RECEIVER.Stop any active wardrive first. Choose AUTO NMEA or the preset below. Match the table: S3 RX GPIO 1, S3 TX GPIO 2 and PPS GPIO 46. If you leave GPS RX or PPS disconnected, select NOT WIRED for that connection. Enable GPS, then tap SAVE / APPLY.
  3. Check the fix before Start.Attach the receiver's suitable antenna and give it a clear view of the sky. Open Scan → Wardriving, turn GPS: ON, and wait for GPS DATA > FIX + UTC / WIGLE CSV. Valid location and UTC are required to save geotagged rows.

CHECK YOUR RECEIVER

Other UART NMEA receivers

AUTO or MANUAL NMEA

Use a voltage-compatible UART output with checksum-valid NMEA RMC and GGA sentences, 8N1, and no flow control. USB-only, I²C-only and binary-only output will not work on these pins.

RX and PPS support in rev144

The table shows the firmware's default assignments: S3 TX GPIO2 → GPS RX and GPS PPS → GPIO46. Rev144 receives NMEA only; it does not send receiver commands or use PPS timing. These two connections are optional for GPS reception. GPIO46 is input-only and cannot serve as S3 TX.

Receiver voltage, baud and troubleshooting

Power and logic are separate. Use the voltage specified for your exact breakout and a suitable UART level shifter whenever signal levels do not match. Bare LC29H modules use a 3.3 V nominal supply but a 2.8 V UART1; their 1.8 V UART2 is a different interface. Use the breakout's NMEA UART output. A board marked “5 V VIN” may have a regulator without UART level conversion.

AUTO listens; it does not reprogram the module. It tries 115200, 9600, 38400, 57600, 19200, 4800, 230400, 460800 and 921600 baud. MANUAL NMEA selects one of those rates. Make sure the receiver is already sending RMC/GGA at the chosen rate.

NO RECENT NMEA: check power, ground, GPS TX → S3 RX, signal levels, selected GPIO, baud and NMEA output. NMEA / NO FIX: data is arriving; check antenna placement and allow time outdoors. FIX / UTC UNVERIFIED: check that the receiver emits valid RMC date/time. The firmware cannot create missing GPS data.

05 / FIELD GUIDE

Before you connect.

A LITTLE PREP GOES A LONG WAY
01—

Bring the right board.

Production Cerberus with an ESP32-S3, 16 MB flash, 2 MB PSRAM and onboard C5. No expansion board is required. Images are checked against the selected processor before writing.

02—

Give it a few minutes.

The guided flow installs a temporary bridge, the C5 radio, then the S3 application. Select the same board when USB reconnects. The radio step takes several minutes.

03—

Keep your session yours.

USB diagnostics, board identifiers and progress stay in your browser unless you save a report. Reports include board MAC addresses. The installer does not upload board reports or radio captures.
